01

Why a strategy first

The expensive mistake is not choosing the wrong model. It is automating the wrong process, in the wrong order, without a way to measure it.

Language models and AI agents can now read, write, decide and act inside your systems. That makes almost every process a candidate, which is exactly the problem: teams start with whatever demo looked good, pilots multiply, and nothing reaches production because nobody agreed on value, ownership or risk.

An AI strategy fixes the order of operations. We map where time and money actually go in your company, score the candidate use cases on value, feasibility and risk, and sequence them into a roadmap that starts with quick, visible wins and builds toward the workflows that change your cost structure.

The result is deliberately short. Executives can read it in an hour, and engineers can start building from it the following week.

  • DurationTypically 4–6 weeks
  • ForFounders, boards, C-level, department heads
  • OutcomeRanked use cases, architecture, governance, business case

02

What you get

Every engagement ends with the same five artefacts. They are written to be used, not filed.

i.

Use-case portfolio

Twenty to forty candidate workflows across marketing, sales, operations, finance, HR and more, each scored on value, effort, data readiness and regulatory risk.

ii.

Prioritised roadmap

Three horizons: what to ship in the next 90 days, what to build over 12 months, and what needs data or organisational groundwork first. With owners, dependencies and decision points.

iii.

Target architecture

Which models, agent frameworks and platforms fit your stack, where data lives, how agents connect to your ERP, CRM and ticketing systems, and what stays in the EU.

iv.

Governance model

Roles, approval paths, human-in-the-loop checkpoints and a risk classification under the EU AI Act, so legal and works council questions are answered before they block a launch.

v.

Business case

Hours saved, cycle times, error rates and revenue effects per use case, with the assumptions written down, plus the running costs of models, platforms and operations.

vi.

Operating model

Who owns AI in your company, how teams request and evaluate new agents, what skills you hire for and what you buy in. Small companies get a one-page version.

Frequently asked questions

How is an AI strategy different from an AI readiness assessment?

The readiness assessment is a shorter diagnostic: where do you stand on data, systems, skills and governance, and what are the two or three obvious first use cases. The strategy goes further: a full use-case portfolio, a target architecture, a governance model and a business case, sequenced into a roadmap. Many clients start with the assessment and extend into a strategy.

Do we need a lot of data or a data science team first?

No. Most agentic use cases run on the documents, emails, tickets and ERP records you already have. Where data quality genuinely blocks a use case, the roadmap says so and includes the data foundations work needed. You do not need a data science team to start; you need one process owner who cares.

Which departments should we start with?

The ones with high volumes of repetitive, document-heavy or communication-heavy work and a measurable outcome: customer service, order processing, accounts payable, sales development, HR helpdesks, contract review. The strategy ranks your specific candidates rather than applying a generic list. Our solutions by department pages show typical starting points.

How do you handle GDPR and the EU AI Act in the strategy?

Each use case gets a preliminary risk classification and a note on the personal data involved. The governance model defines who approves what, where humans stay in the loop and how you document decisions. This is not legal advice, but it means your legal and data-protection teams get a structured input instead of a surprise.

Can you also implement the roadmap?

Yes. That is the point. Our agentic workflow and agent development services deliver the first use cases, and managed AI operations keeps them reliable afterwards. You are equally free to implement with your own team or another partner; the roadmap is written so that you can.

How much time does our team need to invest?

Plan for roughly two to three hours per week for the sponsor and about the same for each department head involved during the mapping phase. We do the analysis, the writing and the prototyping.
