01

What Mittelstand companies actually need

The question is rarely whether to use AI. It is how to get real relief for a stretched team without betting the company on a vendor or a consultancy.

If you are reading this in English: the Mittelstand is the German term for the country's small and mid-sized companies, typically owner-managed or family-owned, often world leaders in a narrow niche, with 50 to 2,000 employees, a lean IT department and an ERP system at the centre of everything. The culture is pragmatic, long-term and sceptical of fashions. We think that is exactly the right attitude toward AI.

The driver is usually not the technology but the skills shortage, the Fachkräftemangel. Order processing, service, accounting and sales support are short-staffed, the experienced people are approaching retirement, and open positions stay open for months. AI agents will not replace those people. They can take the repetitive part of the work off their desks so that the people you have go further, and so that knowledge does not walk out of the door.

We work incrementally: one workflow, one department head who wants it, measurable relief within weeks. We speak German, come on site, involve the works council early and hand over the knowledge so that you stay independent of us. Public funding programmes for digitalisation exist in Germany; we can point you to the relevant ones for your region and size, without promising what they will decide.

02

Where Mittelstand companies usually stand

  • Open positions that stay open

    The service department is missing two technicians, order processing runs on overtime, and the one person who knows the export documentation retires next year. Every process that depends on a single person is a risk, not just a cost.

  • An ERP at the centre and a lean IT team around it

    Everything runs through SAP, Microsoft Dynamics, proALPHA, abas or a comparable system, plus DATEV for accounting and a shared drive full of spreadsheets. IT keeps it all running with a small team and has no capacity for experiments that create new systems to maintain.

  • Pressure to "do something with AI", scepticism about how

    The advisory board asks, the trade association runs a seminar, a competitor shows a chatbot at the trade fair. At the same time, the owner has seen enough software projects overrun to want proof before commitment.

  • Data protection and the works council are part of the culture

    Personal data of customers and employees, the GDPR and codetermination are not obstacles to be worked around. They are part of how the company operates, and an AI project that ignores them will not survive its first works council meeting.

  • Independence matters more than the newest tool

    Nobody wants a second dependency on a vendor or a consultancy that has to be called every time something changes. Whatever is built must be understood, documented and maintainable by the company's own people.

05

Frequently asked questions

We have a lean IT department. Can we even do this?

Yes, and that is the normal case. Most workflows connect to your ERP through existing interfaces and run on platforms your IT does not have to build. The IT effort in a pilot is typically a few days for access and review. We write the documentation so that IT can maintain the result.

Do we have to move our data to the cloud or to a US provider?

Not necessarily. Models can run on EU-hosted infrastructure, in a German data centre or, for some use cases, on your own hardware. We select the option that fits your data, your customers' requirements and your budget. Read more on sovereign AI and EU hosting.

How do you involve the works council?

Early and openly. We prepare a plain-language description of what the agent does, which data it processes and how employees are affected, and we join the meeting if that helps. In our experience the works council supports projects that relieve people and rejects projects it hears about last.

Are there subsidies for this?

Germany and the federal states run funding programmes for digitalisation and AI in small and mid-sized companies. Whether your company qualifies depends on size, region and the type of project. We can point you to the relevant programmes and help with the technical description, but we make no promises about approval.

Which processes pay off first in the Mittelstand?

Order intake and confirmation, quote preparation, service and maintenance knowledge, accounts payable and dunning, supplier communication, and internal helpdesks for HR and IT. See our pages on manufacturing and operations for typical examples.
