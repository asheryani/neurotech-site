Industries

The same technology, very different constraints.

An agent that processes orders in a machine builder's ERP and an agent that triages claims at an insurer share most of their architecture. What differs is regulation, systems, data and the people who have to trust them. These pages cover what changes by industry.

Frequently asked questions

Do you only work with the industries listed here?

No. The listed industries are where we have the most to say. The department-level patterns apply broadly, and the constraints of regulated sectors are usually well documented. If in doubt, describe your situation and we will be honest about fit.

How do industry regulations affect what an agent may do?

Mostly by moving the human checkpoint. In financial services, credit and pricing decisions stay with people and are documented; in manufacturing, drawings and export-controlled data constrain hosting; in professional services, professional secrecy constrains where data may be processed. Our governance service maps these constraints before anything is built.

Do you work with our industry-specific software?

Agents connect to systems through APIs, files, email and, increasingly, the Model Context Protocol. Most industry software can be integrated at one of those levels. During the readiness assessment we check the concrete systems and say where integration is easy, where it needs work and where it is not worth it.
