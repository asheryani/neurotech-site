AI agents for sales: less admin, more conversations.

Sales reps spend a large part of their week on research, data entry and drafting instead of talking to customers. AI agents take over the preparation and the paperwork: they qualify inbound leads, write account briefs, update the CRM from calls and draft quotes from your price list, while your team approves every message that goes to a prospect.

01

Where agents pay off in sales

The bottleneck in most sales teams is not the pipeline. It is the hours between conversations: research, follow-ups, CRM updates and proposals that arrive a day late.

An AI agent in sales reads what arrives (forms, emails, call transcripts), looks things up (CRM history, product data, public sources) and prepares work for a rep: a qualification note, a brief, a draft reply, a CRM update, a quote. It acts inside defined boundaries and stops wherever money or the customer relationship is at stake.

Why now: models have become reliable at extracting facts from messy text and summarising long histories, CRM platforms expose the interfaces agents need, and buyers expect an answer within hours. In Germany and Austria, outreach is also subject to competition and consent rules, which is a reason to design compliance into the flow from day one, not an argument against automation.

We start where the volume is, inbound handling and CRM upkeep, keep a person on every outbound message in the early phases, and measure what sales leaders care about: speed to lead, meetings booked and rep hours returned.

02

Use cases

Six sales workflows where agents do the preparation and people do the selling.

i.

Inbound lead qualification and routing

The agent reads forms, emails and chat transcripts, enriches the company from permitted sources, scores fit against your ideal customer profile and routes the lead to the right rep with a short summary and a suggested reply. The rep approves or edits within minutes; ambiguous leads go to human triage, not to a guess.

ii.

Account research and pre-call briefs

Before a meeting the agent compiles CRM history, open tickets, previous quotes, recent news and the company website into a one-page brief with suggested questions and known risks. Reps read it on the way to the call. The brief is internal; nothing goes to the customer.

iii.

Outbound sequencing with approved messaging

For target accounts the agent drafts personalised messages from templates your team has approved, proposes timing and channel and tracks replies. A rep approves each message or batch before it is sent. We design the flow around German and Austrian rules for unsolicited outreach (UWG, consent): permitted channels, warm triggers, clean opt-outs, confirmed with your legal counsel.

iv.

CRM updates from calls and emails

After a call or an email thread the agent proposes a structured update: next steps, stakeholders, objections, competitor mentions, a stage suggestion and the follow-up task. The rep confirms with one click. Weekly, it flags stale opportunities, missing close dates and single-threaded deals to the sales manager.

v.

Quotes, proposals and tender responses

From product data, price lists and past proposals the agent drafts quotes in your format and validates prices against CPQ or ERP rules. For RFPs and public tenders it parses the requirements, drafts answers from an approved knowledge base and marks every gap it could not fill. A sales lead approves before anything leaves.

vi.

Deal-risk signals and sales knowledge assistant

The agent watches the pipeline for risk patterns (no activity, slipping dates, missing decision maker) and briefs managers before the forecast call. A knowledge assistant answers reps' questions on products, pricing rules and competitors with sources from playbook and product documentation.

03

Worked example

From inbound lead to first meeting

A typical scenario: a software or industrial company receives demo requests and contact-form enquiries daily, and the first reply currently takes a day or two. An SDR agent with a human hand-off changes the timeline like this:

  1. Intake. A prospect fills the contact form or writes to sales@. The agent reads the message and checks the CRM: existing account, open opportunity, previous conversations, current owner.
  2. Enrichment. It adds company facts from permitted sources: size, industry, locations, technology hints from the website. It records business contact data only.
  3. Qualification. It scores fit and writes a note: likely need, urgency, budget signals, suggested owner. Below the threshold the lead goes to a nurture queue a person reviews weekly; unclear cases go to human triage.
  4. Reply draft (human gate). It drafts a reply in the rep's tone with two or three meeting slots from the rep's calendar. The rep or SDR reviews and sends. In the first phase there is no autonomous sending to prospects.
  5. Scheduling. When the prospect answers, the agent handles the back-and-forth within rules and books the meeting, once the flow has proven itself; before that, a person clicks.
  6. Pre-call brief. Twenty-four hours before the meeting the rep receives the brief: context, questions to ask, relevant reference types, pricing boundaries.
  7. After the call. Transcript or notes become a proposed CRM update and the follow-up task; the rep confirms. If an account executive takes over, the hand-off note comes from the same record.
  8. Weekly review. Speed to lead, qualification accuracy on a human-checked sample, meeting rate and rep hours saved are reviewed with the sales lead, and thresholds are adjusted.

Every message to a prospect passes a person in the first phase. Once draft acceptance is consistently high, you decide, step by step, what the agent may send on its own, with full logs and a way to stop it. 04

Guardrails and risks in sales

  • No autonomous sending in early phases

    Drafts, not dispatches. The agent prepares messages, a rep approves them and every send is logged. Autonomy is granted later per step and per segment, based on measured acceptance rates, and can be withdrawn at any time.

  • Outreach law and consent

    In Germany, unsolicited advertising by email generally requires prior consent, also in B2B, and phone outreach to businesses has its own rules under the UWG. We design flows around permitted channels and triggers and recommend confirming the setup with your legal counsel. Process design, not legal advice.

  • Data protection

    CRM records, call transcripts and enrichment data are personal data under the GDPR. We define purpose, sources, retention and access before the pilot, keep processing in the EU where required, and note that recording calls needs the consent of everyone on the line.

  • Pricing accuracy

    The agent never invents a price or a discount. Quotes are built from the price list and contract terms in your CPQ or ERP, validated against rules and approved above defined thresholds by a sales lead. Every number has a source.

  • Adoption and trust

    Agents that reps do not use are worthless. We design with the reps, keep the workflow in their tools and use activity data for coaching only, in agreement with your works council where one exists.

Frequently asked questions

Can the agent send emails to prospects on its own?

Not at the start, and later only where you decide so. In the pilot every prospect-facing message is approved by a rep. Once draft acceptance is consistently high, you can allow autonomous sending for defined steps such as scheduling confirmations, with logs and a stop switch. Cold outreach stays within the legal limits described above.

Is AI-supported cold outreach even allowed in Germany?

Automation does not change the rules, it only makes breaking them faster. Unsolicited email advertising generally requires prior consent, also in B2B, phone outreach has its own rules under the UWG, and the GDPR applies to all contact data. We design flows around permitted channels and triggers and recommend confirming the setup with your legal counsel; we do not give legal advice.

How does the agent get into our CRM? We use Salesforce and Microsoft Dynamics.

Through the platform's APIs or the Model Context Protocol, with a dedicated technical user and the minimum permissions for the use case. We start with read access, move to proposed changes that a rep confirms and only then allow limited direct writes. HubSpot, Pipedrive and other CRMs work the same way.

What about data protection for call transcripts and lead data?

Recording calls requires consent from everyone on the call; transcripts are personal data and need a purpose, a retention period and access rules; enrichment uses only permitted sources. We document all of this before the pilot, keep processing in the EU where required and involve your data-protection officer early. The legal assessment stays with your counsel.

Will our SDRs and reps lose their jobs?

Agents remove tasks, not usually roles. Research, data entry and first drafts shrink; conversations, negotiation and relationship work grow. The SDR role changes most, towards reviewing, judging and coaching the agent, and we plan that change openly with sales leadership rather than letting it happen by accident.
